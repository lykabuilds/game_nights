Wingspan Rules

Attract birds to your wildlife preserve. Each bird you play makes one of your three habitats stronger, so your turns get better as the game goes on. Most points after 4 rounds wins.

You place a cube on the goal board every round, even if you score 0. That's why you get fewer turns each round.

End of each round

  1. Score the round's goal on the goal board with one of your cubes.
  2. Take all action cubes back off your player mat.
  3. Discard the 3 face-up birds in the tray and deal 3 new ones.
  4. Pass the first-player token clockwise.

Each habitat row holds at most 5 birds, filled from left to right.

Paying food
  • Pay the food shown in the bird's top-left corner from your personal supply.
  • A wild food icon can be paid with any food type.
  • Any 2 food tokens can be spent as 1 food of any type.
  • Some birds show “/” between foods: pay either one.
The birdfeeder and dice
  • Taking food means taking a die out of the birdfeeder. Dice stay out until the feeder is rerolled.
  • If the birdfeeder is empty, reroll all 5 dice.
  • If every die left shows the same face, even just 1 die, you may reroll all 5 dice before you take food. You can do this partway through gaining food too.
  • The invertebrate/seed face counts as its own face, and you can take it as either food.
Bird powers: white, brown and pink
  • White, “When played”: happens once, right after you play the bird.
  • Brown, “When activated”: happens each time you use that bird's habitat row. Activate them right to left. Each is optional.
  • Pink, “Once between turns”: triggers during another player's turn, at most once between your own turns.
Caching food and tucking cards
  • Cached food: food placed on a bird card. It can't be spent, but it's worth 1 point each at game end.
  • Tucked cards: cards slid under a bird, for example by flocking birds. Each is worth 1 point.
Egg limit

Each bird shows how many eggs it can hold. If every bird is full, extra eggs from the lay-eggs action are lost. Every egg on your birds is worth 1 point at the end.

Bonus cards

You start with 1 and some birds give you more. Each card scores at game end if you meet its condition, for example points per bird of a certain kind. You can keep your bonus cards secret.

Blue goal board (friendlier)

Score 1 point per target item, up to 5 points, no matter what others have.

Managing the bird deck

If the deck runs out, shuffle the discards to form a new deck. Refill the tray at the end of each turn, not in the middle of drawing.

FAQ quick hits
  • Can I skip brown powers? Yes, each one is optional.
  • Is there a hand or food limit? No.
  • Can I move birds? Not normally. Only bird powers can do that.
  • Can I play a bird in a habitat it doesn't show? No.
  • Can I use a die face for food it doesn't show? No. Use the 2-for-1 substitution when paying instead.

5Bird appendix

Loading birds…

Bird facts come from the community Wingspan card spreadsheet by TawnyFrogmouth on BoardGameGeek, via the open-source Wingsearch project. Exact power wording is on each card. For tricky rulings, check the appendix in your box.

Power colors explained
  • White, when played: happens once, right after you play the bird.
  • Brown, when activated: happens each time you take the action of the row the bird sits in. Brown powers go right to left and are optional.
  • Pink, once between turns: reacts to something another player does, at most once before your next turn.
  • Teal, round end (European Expansion): happens at the end of every round.
  • Yellow, game end (Oceania Expansion): happens once when the game ends, before scoring.
Predators and hunting

Predators try to catch prey. Usually you reveal a card from the deck and check its wingspan, or roll dice not in the birdfeeder. If the hunt works, you tuck the card behind the predator or cache food on it. Each tucked card or cached food is worth 1 point at the end.

Flocking and tucking

Flocking birds let you tuck cards behind them, often from your hand. A tucked card is no longer a bird you can play, but it's worth 1 point. Some flocking powers also give you something back, like a card or food.

Caching food

Cached food sits on a bird card. You can't spend it, but each token is worth 1 point at game end.

Nest types

There are four nest types: bowl, cavity, ground and platform. A star nest counts as any nest type, for bonus cards, goals and powers. A few birds have no nest and can't hold eggs.

Food cost symbols
  • Several food icons in a row: pay all of them.
  • Icons split by a /: pay any one of them.
  • Wild: pay any one food.
  • Remember: any 2 food can stand in for 1 food of any type.
Wingspan size

The number in centimeters on each card. It matters for predator hunts and some bonus cards. A few flightless birds show a star wingspan, which counts as any size.

Nectar (Oceania Expansion)

Nectar is an extra food type that can be paid as any food. Unspent nectar is discarded at the end of each round. At game end, players compare how much nectar they spent in each habitat for bonus points.

6Game end and winning

The game ends…

…after round 4, once everyone has used their 5 turns and the last goal is scored.

Scoring

  • Points printed on your birds
  • Bonus cards you completed
  • End-of-round goals
  • 1 point per egg, cached food and tucked card

Tie: the player with the most unused food tokens wins. If still tied, they share the win.
